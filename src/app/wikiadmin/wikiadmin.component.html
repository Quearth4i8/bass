TRIX Formula & the Weiss (1970) Oxygen Saturation Equation

How the four TRIX inputs are derived from raw measurements, and how measured O₂ is converted into a saturation deviation using Weiss (1970).

Used on Overview
DIN — Dissolved Inorganic Nitrogen
DIN = (NH₄ + NO₃ + NO₂) × 14

Ammonium + nitrate + nitrite (µmol/L), converted to µg/L N.

DIP — Dissolved Inorganic Phosphorus
DIP = PO₄ × 31

Phosphate (µmol/L), converted to µg/L P.

|ΔO₂%| — Weiss (1970) oxygen solubility, S = 36 PSU (fixed)
ln(O₂sat) = −173.4292 + 249.6339·(100/T) + 143.3483·ln(T/100) − 21.8492·(T/100) + S·(−0.033096 + 0.014259·(T/100) − 0.001700·(T/100)²)

T is water temperature in Kelvin (°C + 273.15), S is salinity (PSU). O₂sat comes out in mL/L and is converted to mg/L by × 1.4276 (O₂ density at STP).

DO% = (O₂ / O₂sat) × 100   →   |ΔO₂%| = | DO% − 100 |

O₂ is the measured dissolved oxygen (mg/L). The resulting |ΔO₂%| is the DO2 term fed into the TRIX formula below — it is the absolute deviation of the water's actual oxygen level from what full saturation at that temperature would be.

TRIX = ( log₁₀( DIN × DIP × |ΔO₂%| × Chla ) + 1.5 ) / 1.2

Chla is chlorophyll-a (µg/L), measured directly. The result is clamped to the 0–10 range used by the Overview gauges.
